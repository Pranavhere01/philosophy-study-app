Most graduate admissions run on judgement, and judgement rewards pedigree. A minority of countries run the opposite way: they publish a marking scheme, sit you an examination or interview, add up the points, and admit on the total. This site catalogues 65 programmes across 48 countries, marking every one for whether an exam or a supervisor decides.

Fixed constraint: a coursework master's, with no dissertation

Several of the strongest routes in this guide require a research master's with a thesis. With a coursework-only master's those close before grades are ever considered, and the constraint is not fixable retroactively. So this site marks them closed and ranks only what remains.

Closed on this ground: Quebec's PBEEE, which states outright that an essay or research report is not accepted · Tunisia, where a research-based master's is mandatory · Israel, which requires a thesis-track degree with the thesis graded at 85 · Australia's Research Training Program, which wants first-class honours or a research master's · New Zealand · and any route that demands an MPhil.

At risk, and flagged on their pages: Lithuania, which weights the final master's thesis grade directly into its formula · Sapienza, which asks for a second-cycle qualification "with a written final thesis" · and the United States, where the writing sample is the decisive document and a thesis is the obvious source of one.

What survives is decided by a research project and an interview — a proposal you write, not a credential you lack. That is the pivot this guide now takes, and it is why Poland, Czechia and Estonia sit at the top: all three judge a submitted project, and none of them asks whether the master's carried a thesis.

The distinction that decides everything: is it your transcript, and which transcript?

Some systems score the applicant's academic record against a national cut-off; others do not. That alone reorders the list. But there is a sharper split inside the first group, and with a strong master's it decides the outcome.

Systems that score the MASTER'S record are cleared by a 75%+ result. Converting 75% through the modified Bavarian formula gives a German grade of roughly 2.15 to 2.5 depending on the pass mark — which meets the published doctoral floors at FU Berlin, LMU and Heidelberg, meets KU Leuven's hard 75% gate, clears Greece's 8/10 minimum, South Africa's 70% aggregate and the NRF's 65%, satisfies Botswana's 3.5/5, and meets ELTE's “good” classification requirement.

Systems that score the BACHELOR'S record — or that exclude the master's from scoring altogether — are not helped at all by a strong master's. This is the trap, and it is where the two groups must not be lumped together. Spain's FPU excludes master's and doctoral study from the scored average by rule, so the score comes from the bachelor's against a published humanities cut-off of 8.230. Chile's ANID is the same: 30% of the score is the undergraduate average and postgraduate grades are explicitly inadmissible. Italy's titoli grid and Portugal's FCT criterion still carry the first degree, so a strong master's lifts them only partly.

Systems that score neither remain open regardless, and are where the shortlist should concentrate: Charles University's oral has no grade component at all, Poland scores the first degree at zero, and Germany, Estonia and the Nordics hire on a supervisor's judgement.

The principle, in one number

Poland's Adam Mickiewicz University awards a maximum of 3 points out of 100 to the master's grade and zero points to the first degree, while the interview alone carries 50. Systems like that are engineered to be indifferent to where you studied — and Polish doctoral schools are tuition-free while paying a stipend.

What actually blocks these routes

Not the entrance examination — almost none of these are written subject tests. Two other things bind: whether the system scores your transcript (see above), and recognition of the degree itself. Several countries require a formal nostrification or equivalence certificate before you may even enrol, and some exclude distance-mode degrees outright. The equivalence machinery is documented almost everywhere — Portugal's DL 66/2018, Czech nostrification, Italy's CIMEA statement, Greece's NARIC registry, Poland's KWALIFIKATOR — but no country publishes whether it accepts an open-university distance master's. Ask in writing, before paying anything.

A third factor matters more than grades: a publication record. Warsaw awards 40 of 100 points to documented research activity, Maribor weights it at 60%, and Charles admitted one applicant in four.

Countries with a lower standard of living than India, measured by the UNDP Human Development Index (India is 0.685, rank 130 of 193), are excluded from this site — so Ethiopia, Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda, Rwanda, Ghana, Nigeria, Senegal, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Nepal, Myanmar, Cambodia and Laos do not appear. Sri Lanka, Mauritius, Egypt, Morocco, Tunisia and Algeria do.

How to read the funding labels

LabelMeaning
FundedTuition covered and a stipend or salary paid, on the terms shown on the programme page.
Partial / conditionalReal funding exists but is conditional — a co-financing requirement, a hard grade floor, few places, or language conditions.
BlockedClosed for documented reasons: a qualification-recognition exclusion, a published grade floor, or a scholarship field-of-study restriction.
