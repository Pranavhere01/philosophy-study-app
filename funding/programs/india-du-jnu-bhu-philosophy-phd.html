Details

How it converts
UGC's notice of 27 March 2024 created three categories, effective from 2024-25. Category 1 (JRF-qualified) is admitted on interview only. Categories 2 and 3 receive 70% weightage for the NET score and 30% for interview.
How competitive it actually is
June 2025 Philosophy cut-offs, out of 300: the unreserved JRF cut-off was 222 and only 12 candidates in the entire subject qualified for it. The PhD-only cut-off was 166, with 402 qualifiers. So the JRF bar is roughly 74% and the pool of unreserved Philosophy JRF holders is tiny — while the PhD-only route is far easier but pays no stipend whatsoever.
The realistic landing zone
Plan for the PhD-only category plus a university or ICSSR fellowship rather than assuming JRF. ICSSR pays ₹20,000/month and is mandatory-NET, which makes the same exam serve both.
Validity
NET marks are valid for PhD admission for one year only. A December 2026 result therefore buys the 2027-28 admission cycle.
Delhi University
PhD Phase I is open only to NET/JRF holders, along with CSIR-NET, AYUSH-NET, DBT-JRF, ICMR-JRF, DST-INSPIRE, GATE and teacher fellows. Phase II is the entrance-test route.
JNU
Adopts the UGC notice wholesale — all three NET categories, with the NET percentile replacing the entrance examination. Note it counts only June 2025-onward percentiles.
an open university
Offers no Philosophy PhD. The only open an open university doctorate is PHDITS in Interdisciplinary and Transdisciplinary Studies, which accepts UGC NET in Philosophy among its listed subjects.
ICSSR as a second route
The ICSSR Full-Term Doctoral Fellowship pays ₹20,000/month plus ₹20,000/year contingency under Domain 28, “Socio-Philosophical Studies”. UGC NET is mandatory for it and there is no written test. It cannot be stacked with JRF or ICPR.
IIT Delhi is blocked
HSS requires 60% marks or 6.00 CGPA for M.A. and B.Tech entrants, which a 2:2-equivalent bachelor's does not meet, even though NET is also required.

Syllabus & curriculum

UGC NET Philosophy syllabus — subject code 03

Ten units: classical Indian epistemology and metaphysics, classical Western philosophy, Indian ethics, Western ethics, contemporary Indian philosophy, recent Western philosophy, social and political philosophy (Indian and Western), logic, and applied philosophy. Paper II is 100 questions for 200 marks.
