Details

How the concorso works
You submit qualifications — a degree certificate stating the minimum and maximum grade of your system, a thesis abstract, a CV, and a research project which may be in English — and sit an oral. Sapienza's philosophy call: titoli out of 75 with a minimum of 55 to reach the oral, then the oral out of 50 with 35 to pass. Eight places, six funded, and eighteen candidates were classified.
The titoli problem
The grid scores both the master's mark and the first-cycle degree mark — Sapienza explicitly, and Bologna allocates 10 of its 50 titoli points to both degree marks. Publications count only if already published in ANVUR-listed journals. A weak bachelor's with no publications scores at the floor and may not clear the titoli gate to reach the oral at all.
What is genuinely favourable
No minimum grade is published, the call reads “senza limiti di età e cittadinanza”, the oral may be conducted entirely in English, and remote orals are permitted — explicitly by Zoom on request at Padova, and at Sapienza's committee's discretion.
Master's fees, which vary enormously
Sapienza charges a non-EU resident abroad a flat €300 a year, and it is the cheapest place in Europe to test whether a distance master's is accepted. Padova charges a fixed, non-recalculable €2,790 a year — India is not on the “particularly poor countries” list that would reduce it. Bologna is income-banded and can reach €0, though €157.04 is added regardless.
The borsa
€16,243–€18,855 gross a year depending on the university — Politecnico di Torino verified €18,854.52 for both years, about €1,386 net a month. It is income-tax exempt but subject to social security contributions, and research periods abroad carry an uplift of at least 50%.
