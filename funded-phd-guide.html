1. The brute-force principle

Most of the world's prestigious graduate admissions run on judgement: a committee reads your file, weighs your institution, and decides. That process rewards pedigree, and a distance master's from a distance university is a handicap inside it.

A minority of countries run the opposite way. They publish a marking scheme, sit you an examination, add up the points, and admit on the total. These are the routes where your undergraduate CGPA is a line item at most — and in Poland it is literally worth zero points. That is the entire strategic case for this document.

The single most useful number in this document

Poland's Adam Mickiewicz University awards a maximum of 3 points out of 100 to the master's grade and zero points to the first degree. Fifty of the hundred points come from an interview, twenty from a research project. In other words, the system is engineered to be indifferent to where you studied and how you did as an undergraduate — and it pays you PLN 3,570.50/month (about €840) rising to PLN 5,550.50 with no tuition.

2. Every entrance-exam route, ranked

These are the routes where sitting a test is the way in. Ranked by how favourable the mechanics are for an applicant without a strong undergraduate record.

The honest count: only three of these are genuinely examination-gated

Four regional streams tested the premise that many countries run a doctorate gated by an objective examination rather than a supervisor's judgement. The premise is mostly false, verified against primary sources:

  • Genuinely examination-scored: Italy, Hungary and Czechia — plus Poland partly, since its humanities admission is scored but oral rather than a written subject test.
  • Scored on documents rather than an exam: Spain (FPU/FPI weighted scoring) and Portugal (FCT panel ranking of an application).
  • Not examination-gated at all, despite appearances: Turkey — ALES expressly exempts foreign applicants, and the real gate is YÖK denklik recognition. Greece — no examination and no tuition at all. Romania — a written Doctoral Tutor agreement is required before you apply. France — an école doctorale audition with no national exam. China — the national kaoyan examination is effectively closed to foreigners and doctoral entry has shifted to dossier-based assessment.

The brute-force strategy therefore narrows to a short, specific list — but it is a strong one, and Hungary publishes the best-documented objective bar found anywhere: a pass mark of 56 out of 100.

⚠ Unresolved conflict on Turkey — two research streams disagree

One stream found that Turkish universities exempt foreign applicants from ALES entirely, quoting a university stating that prospective foreign students "are NOT required to submit ALES or an equivalent exam result document". A second stream, working independently, found that ALES is required by most Turkish universities for doctoral entry, alongside a YDS or YÖKDİL language score — which would make Turkey the region's closest thing to a score-decides route.

Both cannot be right, and the likely resolution is that it varies by university. Turkish law delegates the question to each institution's Senate, so some exempt foreigners and others do not. Treat Turkey's exam requirement as university-specific and verify it department by department. Either way, the YÖK denklik recognition of a distance master's remains the real gate.

Two structural gates sit outside the exam in several countries. First, whether a distance-mode master's is recognised at all — Estonia voids an offer on a negative assessment, Portugal scores the curriculum at zero without a DGES certificate, China cannot certify it, and Taiwan excludes it by regulation. Second, language: France's doctorate effectively requires DALF C1, Greece requires Greek with no English fallback, and Japan's entrance exams are usually sat in Japanese.

⭐ A live, funded, English-language philosophy PhD — deadline 15 December 2026

LMU Munich, Chair of Metaphysics (Professor Alyssa Ney), is advertising a doctoral position on an ERC project in the metaphysics of quantum physics:

  • TV-L E13 at 65% — stated as €2,722–3,951/month gross, three years
  • Start 1 October 2027 — which is precisely your timeline, since your M.A. completes in mid-2027
  • Work entirely in English — no German requirement
  • Requires a Master's in philosophy or philosophy of science
  • Deadline 15 December 2026

How to apply: a single PDF containing a motivation letter, CV with certificates, a writing sample (your master's thesis is the obvious candidate), a research-interest statement of no more than three pages, and three referees — sent under the subject line "ERC PhD student application". A physics background is an advantage on this particular project.

One timing tension to handle: the post starts October 2027 but the deadline is December 2026, so you would apply before the M.A. is conferred. That is normal for doctoral applications — but you must be able to evidence that the degree is in progress and on track, which is exactly what Jagiellonian's pending-master's form is designed to do (§2, Poland). Consider obtaining an equivalent statement from the open university.

8. Africa & Middle East

Almost no brute-force routes here

A dedicated stream tested every system in the region against the central question. The answer is that there is almost no true test-decides PhD route in Africa or the Middle East. Nearly every system is a scored dossier or plain departmental discretion. Only a handful are test-gated, and two of those turn out to be traps.

Filtered out: countries with a lower standard of living than India

This survey now excludes destinations whose human development is below India's, on the reasoning that a funded doctorate there is not an improvement in living conditions. The measure used is the UNDP Human Development Index, where India sits at 0.685 — rank 130 of 193 in the 2025 report (2023 data).

Of the ten targets above, only Italy, Hungary and Czechia are genuinely examination-scored (§2). Poland is scored but oral. Estonia and Germany are supervisor-hiring. Portugal and Spain score documents. If an objective test is the priority, the order to work in is Italy → Hungary → Czechia → Poland.

Two things to settle before spending money

One: does the target accept a distance-mode master's? Estonia voids an offer on a negative assessment; Portugal scores the curriculum at zero without a DGES certificate; China cannot certify a distance degree; Taiwan excludes it by regulation. Ask in writing, before any application fee.

Two: your publication record is the real competitive weakness, not the CGPA. Warsaw awards 40 of 100 points to documented research activity, Maribor weights it at 60%, and Charles admitted 1 of 4. A conference paper or an annotated translation is worth more to these applications than any further grade polishing — and unlike grades, you can start producing it today.
