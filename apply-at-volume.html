1. The premise

With no edge assumed, the only lever you fully control is how many funded slots you enter. Expected offers scale with ticket count, not with how cleverly you picked a topic. So: monitor everything → triage in seconds → apply to anything you are formally eligible for. The sections below tell you, route by route, what the committee actually weighs — so your 20 minutes per application are spent on the thing that moves the score.

The one rule

If you meet the formal requirements, you apply. Do not pre-reject yourself on pedigree, on "distance M.A.", or on "only 60% topic fit." Self-rejection is the single biggest funnel leak, and it is exactly what a volume strategy removes.

The honest boundary

Skip only the hard closes: thesis-based master's required · citizenship/visa restriction · a recognition certificate you lack · fees you can't meet. Everything else gets an application.

3. The 60-second eligibility filter

  1. Degree level met? M.A. completed (or completing).
  2. Discipline in scope? Philosophy or adjacent (logic, cognitive science, Indology/South Asian, history of ideas, religious studies).
  3. Medium workable? English, German, or Hindi.
  4. Hard close? thesis-required · citizenship-restricted · recognition missing · fees unaffordable → skip. Otherwise continue.
  5. Apply. 50–60% topic fit is enough.

Pre-verify the thing that can zero you

Degree recognition: Germany (Anabin / uni-assist), Italy (CIMEA), Portugal (DGES), Czechia (nostrification). Get a written answer on a distance-mode M.A. before you invest in a lane.

4. The reusable kit

  • CV — 2 pages + a retargetable "research statement" block.
  • Writing sample — the one thesis-depth paper, used everywhere.
  • Proposal (Exposé) — 1–2 pages, re-aimed per posting in ~30 min.
  • Cover-letter template — 3 paragraphs; swap the middle one.
  • Two referees, pre-briefed.
  • Documents bundle — transcripts, degree, passport, language certs, recognition docs.

One folder, ~20 minutes per application. This is the machine; the weightage map below tells you where to aim each shot.

5. The weightage map — what each route actually scores

Read the table this way

The routes in bold with numbers are the ones where a no-edge applicant converts best: they publish a rubric and score a proposal or an exam, not your institution. Put most of your batch volume there; use Germany and the Anglosphere as the high-volume, low-probability lanes.

6. Germany — supervisor decides (specific openings on this site)

Funding is a supervisor appointment or a DFG project hire — there is no published numeric rubric. What de facto carries it: the writing sample, the proposal, publications, and fit to the professor's project. German widens the salaried pool (teaching duties); analytic clusters run in English.

  • LMU Munich — Metaphysics (ERC project) — €2,722–3,951/month gross, 3 years
  • LMU Munich — Philosophy — 65% of €4,759.37 ≈ €3,094/month gross
  • Humboldt University Berlin — TV-L E13 at 50–65%
  • Ruhr-Universität Bochum — TV-L E13 50–65%, DFG research training group
  • Heidelberg University — TV-L E13 50–65%
  • Goethe University Frankfurt — TV-L E13 50–65%; Böll €1,500/month

The proactive German lane

Search DFG GEPRIS for "Philosophie", list the active funded projects, and email each PI a 5-line inquiry + CV. A funded PI who hasn't advertised yet is the cheapest high-value contact in the whole funnel. The DAAD research grant for doctoral candidates (proposal + host letter, ~October deadline) is the most objective German lane, and DAAD GSSP funds internationals in structured programs.

7. English-speaking world

Mostly program-funded and decided on a dossier: writing sample first, then references, statement, grades. No exam. The Anglosphere is the volume lane — many programs, cheap applications, but the most pedigree-sensitive part of the funnel.

  • United States — mid-tier funded departments — funded by default (stipend + tuition + health); GRE varies by department
  • Canada — Quebec (PBEEE) — dossier, mandatory university preselection ~October; $25,000/yr
  • United Kingdom — AHRC studentships — writing sample + references; AHRC covers home fees only (intl gap ~£18,000/yr)
  • Cambridge — Gates Cambridge — 2 writing samples (3,000–5,000 words) + 500-word proposal + Zoom interview; full cost; ~70/yr
  • Ireland — Research Ireland / GOIPG — institutional nomination, no exam; up to €31,000/yr
  • Australia — RTP — research proposal + supervisor; stipend + fee offset
  • New Zealand — doctoral scholarships — supervisor + proposal; internationals pay domestic fees

8. English-medium Europe — salaried jobs (no exam, interview-based)

These hire you as an employee. The gate is an advertised position → CV → interview; grades are a threshold, and fit + publications carry the decision. English is the working language.

  • Netherlands — University of Amsterdam — €3,204–4,051/month + holiday + year-end bonus
  • Sweden — Stockholm University — SEK 32,900→35,000/month
  • Norway — University of Oslo — NOK 550,000–615,000/year
  • Denmark — Aarhus University — DKK 31,845–38,458/month before tax
  • Finland — University of Helsinki — supervisor + doctoral-programme admission
  • Estonia — University of Tartu — no exam, no grade floor, bachelor's unscored; €2,100/month gross

9. Objective-exam Europe — the published-weightage routes

These are the no-edge converters: they score a proposal, a document, or an interview on a published formula, and your institution's name is a line item at most. Most allow an English oral. This is where your batch volume should concentrate.

  • Poland — Adam Mickiewicz, Poznań — 100 pts: master's grade 3 · achievements 22 · other 5 · project 20 · interview 50; pass 60, ≥30 from interview. PLN 3,570.50→5,550.50/month.
  • Poland — University of Warsaw — two-stage: documents + project, then oral; PLN 4,346→5,550.50/month. Publications ≈40/100.
  • Poland — Jagiellonian, Kraków — research plan + single-authored essay + reference + interview; PLN 3,570.50→5,550.50/month.
  • Czechia — Charles University, Prague — single-round oral (EN/FR/DE), no CGPA weighting; CZK 26,880/month.
  • Czechia — Masaryk, Brno — documents (limit 60/90) + oral (limit 70/100); CZK 26,880/month.
  • Hungary — ELTE Budapest (Stipendium Hungaricum) — scored entrance exam, published min 56/100; HUF 140,000→180,000/month.
  • Romania — University of Bucharest — oral colloquium (not written); bursary ~3,700 lei/month (unverified).
  • Italy — Sapienza, Rome — concorso: qualifications + oral, no minimum grade; net ~€1,195/month.
  • Italy — University of Milan — qualifications + oral; €18,400/year + €1,840 research budget.
  • Spain — FPU (national) — 75 pts academic trajectory (hard threshold 60) + 25 team/supervisor. €32,500/year ×4.
  • Spain — UPF Barcelona (ERC) — scored, min 70 pts; ~€18,269.64/year.
  • Portugal — FCT (national) — candidate merit 30% · work plan 40% · host conditions 30%; below 3.000 ineligible. €1,359.64/month.
  • Lithuania — Vilnius — project + interview, published formula, pass 5/10; €1,406→1,628/month.

The full map — including Asia, the Middle East, Africa and Latin America (which need other languages and so sit outside this English/German/Hindi scope) — is at funding/index.html and funding/map.html.

10. India — NET-scored (English / Hindi)

  • DU, JNU, BHU and other central universities — UGC JRF — the NET score replaces the entrance test; ₹37,000→42,000/month for 5 years.

This is the highest-certainty funded lane you already hold a ticket for — keep it as the floor of the funnel while the rest is probabilistic.

11. Weekly rhythm & tracker

Cadence

  • Daily 20–30 min — scan PhilJobs, H-Net, EURAXESS, ScholarshipDB, DAAD; log every eligible posting.
  • Two batch days/week — send 3–5 applications each, using the kit.
  • Monthly — re-run country sweeps for seasonal deadlines (GEPRIS, DAAD, IRC, DTPs).
  • Target — 8–12 applications/month, weighted toward the §9 published-rubric routes.

The meta-rule

Top-of-funnel is a throughput game, and your two real bottlenecks are (1) a writing sample that isn't thesis-depth yet and (2) zero publications. Every week you spend applying without also upgrading those two is a week the funnel stays narrow. Build the sample and get one publication while you apply, not instead of applying.
