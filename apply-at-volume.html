1. The premise

With no edge assumed, the only lever you fully control is how many funded slots you enter. Every application is a lottery ticket with a low individual probability; the expected number of funded offers scales with the number of tickets, not with how cleverly you picked a topic. So the system is: monitor everything → triage in seconds → apply to anything you are formally eligible for.

The one rule

If you meet the formal requirements, you apply. Do not pre-reject yourself on pedigree, on "my M.A. is distance-mode", or on "I only 60% fit the topic." Judging fit is the committee's job — your job is to give them the chance to say yes. Self-rejection is the single biggest funnel leak, and it is exactly the thing a volume strategy is designed to remove.

The honest boundary

Volume is not "apply to everything on Earth." Some slots are hard-closed — they require a thesis-based master's, a specific citizenship, or a degree-recognition certificate you don't have. Those you skip (the filter in §3 catches them). Everything else, you apply to.

3. The 60-second eligibility filter

Run every posting through this in under a minute. It exists to let you apply fast and skip only the hard closes.

  1. Degree level met? M.A. completed (or completing) — yes → continue.
  2. Discipline in scope? Philosophy, or adjacent (logic, cognitive science, Indology/South Asian, history of ideas, religious studies) — yes → continue.
  3. Medium workable? English, German, or Hindi — yes → continue.
  4. Hard close? Any of: thesis-based master's explicitly required · citizenship/visa-restricted · a recognition certificate you lack · fees you can't meet. If a hard close is present → skip. Otherwise → continue.
  5. Apply. Topic fit at ~50–60% is enough. Send it.

Pre-verify the one thing that can zero you

Degree recognition is the silent killer: Germany (Anabin / uni-assist), Italy (CIMEA comparability), Portugal (DGES), Czechia (nostrification). Before you invest in a country lane, get a written answer on whether a distance-mode M.A. is accepted. One email now prevents ten wasted applications later.

4. The reusable application kit

Volume only works if each application is cheap. Assemble these once; then each posting costs you a retargeted cover letter, not a from-scratch dossier.

  • CV — 2 pages, academic, with a clear "research statement" block you retarget.
  • Writing sample — the single thesis-depth paper (§note in the research-areas page): one document, used everywhere.
  • Proposal (Exposé) — a 1–2 page master proposal you re-aim at each posting's theme in 30 minutes.
  • Cover-letter template — 3 paragraphs: what you're applying to, why it fits, what you bring. Swap the middle paragraph.
  • Two referees pre-agreed — briefed and ready to send letters on short notice.
  • Documents bundle — transcripts, degree certificate, passport, language certificates, plus the recognition docs (Anabin/CIMEA) once obtained.

Store these in one folder with clear filenames so a single application takes ~20 minutes, not an afternoon.

5. Germany (German / English)

Germany is the anchor: the largest number of salaried philosophy positions, and funding that does not require you to be admitted to a "program" first — a supervisor hires you or hosts your grant.

  • Salaried positions — search EURAXESS, H-Net, PhilJobs and university job boards for Wissenschaftliche/r Mitarbeiter/in Philosophie, Promotionsstelle, DFG-Projekt Philosophie. Usually 50–75% TV-L E13, rolling, no fixed season.
  • Proactive lane — DFG GEPRIS — search "Philosophie" in GEPRIS, list the active funded projects, note the principal investigator, and email a 5-line inquiry with your CV + one-line project. A funded PI who hasn't yet advertised is the cheapest high-value contact in the whole funnel.
  • DAAD research grant for doctoral candidates — proposal + a host-supervisor letter; stipend for ~1 year (extendable). Deadline typically ~October for the following year. The most objective German lane.
  • DAAD GSSP — structured doctoral programs with DAAD-funded slots for internationals.
  • Clusters / RTGs — Frankfurt Normative Orders, MCMP Munich, Science of Intelligence Berlin, and others: check their "Open positions / Calls" pages.
  • Language — analytic clusters and many structured programs run in English; German C1 widens the salaried-position pool (some teaching duties need it).

Honest note: the Anglosphere is the most pedigree-sensitive part of the funnel. With no edge, treat it as the volume lane (many programs, cheap applications) rather than the highest-probability lane — the objective-exam countries in §8 convert better per application.

7. English-medium Europe (salaried jobs, rolling)

These hire you as an employee or pay a stipend, usually in English, and post year-round.

  • Netherlands — PhD is a 4-year salaried job; AcademicTransfer + EURAXESS + university sites.
  • Nordics — Denmark (university job boards), Norway (Jobbnorge), Sweden (university + EURAXESS); English working language, salaried.
  • Belgium — KU Leuven Institute of Philosophy (English); FWO fellowships (~€2,700/month, no nationality bar).
  • Austria — university positions (analytic depts run in English); OeAD scholarships.
  • Switzerland — university positions; Swiss Government Excellence Scholarship (~Nov deadline).

8. Objective-exam Europe (English oral OK)

These are the "no-edge" converters from the funded guide — they score a proposal or an exam, not your pedigree. Most allow an English oral/English programme, so language is not a barrier.

  • Italy — concorso: no minimum grade, no age/citizenship limit, oral in English (Sapienza, Bologna, Parma, SNS Pisa). Mar–Jun deadlines.
  • Poland — doctoral schools: bachelor's scores zero, interview-heavy, tuition-free + stipend (Poznań, Warsaw, Jagiellonian). Apr–May.
  • Czechia — oral on the proposal, no CGPA weighting (Charles University, Prague).
  • Hungary — Stipendium Hungaricum, published pass mark (56/100), India named at doctoral level (ELTE Budapest).
  • Romania — entrance examination, can be sat online.

9. India (English / Hindi)

  • UGC NET JRF — the funded route: ₹37,000→42,000/month for five years; the NET score replaces the entrance test at DU, JNU, BHU and other central universities. Medium is English or Hindi.
  • This is the highest-certainty funded lane you already hold a ticket for (your NET sitting) — keep it as the floor of the funnel while the rest is probabilistic.

10. The weekly rhythm & tracker

Cadence

  • Daily, 20–30 min — scan PhilJobs, H-Net, EURAXESS, ScholarshipDB, DAAD. Log every eligible posting.
  • Two batch days a week — send 3–5 applications per batch using the kit (§4).
  • Monthly — re-run the country sweeps (GEPRIS, DAAD database, IRC, DTPs) for seasonal deadlines.
  • Target — 8–12 applications/month. A hundred low-probability tickets beats five perfect ones when you assume no edge.

The meta-rule

Top-of-funnel is a throughput game. Your two real bottlenecks are not the topic and not the language — they are (1) a writing sample that isn't thesis-depth yet, and (2) zero publications. Every week you spend only applying without also upgrading those two is a week the funnel stays narrow. Build the sample and get one publication while you apply, not instead of applying.
